SMART Goal Help

SMART Goal & Action Step Examples

Use this page when you need an idea, want to check whether your SMART goal is specific enough, or need help turning a vague action step into something your team can actually carry out and document.

One goal, two standards, one story

For STAR’s 2026–27 Point-of-Service Action Plan, your one SMART goal should address both Safe & Supportive Environment (SSE) and Youth Voice & Leadership (YVL).

The examples on this page are starting points. Use your own site feedback and needs to choose a focus, then adjust the measure, frequency, and activities so the plan makes sense for your students and program.

First: know the difference between a goal and an action step

This is one of the easiest places for an Action Plan to get confusing.

SMART Goal = the result

Your SMART goal explains what you want to improve, how you will recognize progress, and the timeframe for the improvement.

Think: What should be different for students or the program by the end of the year?

Action Steps = how you will get there

Your 3–5 action steps describe the specific things your team will do to work toward the SMART goal.

Think: What will someone actually do, who will be responsible, and when will it happen?

Site Feedback or Data → SSE + YVL → One SMART Goal → 3–5 Specific Action Steps → Person + Timeline + Resources → Evidence

Ideas for a SMART goal that connects SSE and YVL

Browse by focus area. Each category includes one model goal and several possible action ideas.

Do not copy the action ideas into the SMART Goal field. The model goal describes the improvement you are working toward. The action ideas are possible ways to make that improvement happen.
SMART does not mean long.

A strong goal can often fit in one sentence: By [date], strengthen [SSE + YVL focus] through [measurable target]. That can still cover all five parts: a clear focus (S), a measurable target (M), a realistic target for your site (A), a connection to SSE + YVL and your data (R), and a deadline (T).

Student Voice, Feedback, and Trust

Help students feel safe speaking up and make sure their ideas and concerns are heard.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, strengthen student voice and trust through two feedback opportunities each month and one documented student-informed improvement each quarter.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Hold one student feedback circle each month and document at least one program improvement each quarter based on student feedback.
  • Give students a short monthly survey and use the results to make at least three program improvements during the year.
  • Provide students with at least two opportunities each month to share ideas or concerns and document how staff responded.
  • Establish a student suggestion system, review suggestions monthly, and implement at least four student-generated ideas during the year.
  • Conduct monthly student check-ins about safety, inclusion, and belonging and document at least one response or improvement each month.

Student Choice and Inclusive Activities

Give students meaningful choices while keeping activities safe, welcoming, accessible, and responsive to student interests.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, strengthen student choice and inclusion by using student input to shape one activity each month and making one documented adjustment each quarter.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Give students a choice between at least two activity options twice each month and document student choices.
  • Hold a monthly student vote to help select upcoming activities and use the results when planning.
  • Have students help choose or plan at least one activity each month and document how their input influenced the activity.
  • Ask students about their activity interests once each quarter and use their feedback to add or adjust at least one activity each quarter.
  • Conduct a quarterly student review of activities and make at least one documented change each quarter based on student feedback about choice, safety, or inclusion.

Student Leadership Roles

Give students real opportunities to lead, contribute, and take responsibility in ways that feel supported and meaningful.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, strengthen student leadership and belonging through at least two supported leadership opportunities each month, with participation documented.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Provide at least two student leadership opportunities each month and document student participation.
  • Establish a student leadership team that meets at least once each month and helps plan or improve the program.
  • Have students lead or co-lead at least one program activity each month and collect brief feedback about the experience.
  • Create at least four meaningful student leadership roles and rotate students through those roles during the year.
  • Give older students opportunities to serve as peer mentors or activity helpers at least twice each month and conduct regular staff check-ins about how the roles are working.

Belonging, Inclusion, and Fair Treatment

Help students feel respected, included, and comfortable being themselves while giving them a voice in improving program climate.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, strengthen belonging and fair treatment through quarterly student feedback and at least three documented student-informed improvements.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Hold monthly student check-ins about belonging and inclusion and make at least one improvement each quarter based on student feedback.
  • Have students and staff create shared program expectations together, review them once each quarter, and document student feedback.
  • Ask students about fairness, inclusion, and belonging once each quarter and make at least three documented improvements based on their responses.
  • Provide one monthly discussion or activity focused on belonging, respect, or inclusion and document student suggestions for improving the program.
  • Use quarterly student feedback to identify concerns about inclusion or fair treatment and document how staff responded to those concerns.

Student-Centered Behavior and Problem-Solving

Give students a voice in creating expectations, identifying concerns, and helping solve problems that affect the program community.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, strengthen student voice and shared responsibility through one problem-solving opportunity each month and one documented student-informed solution each quarter.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Have students and staff develop program behavior expectations together, review them once each quarter, and make at least one documented adjustment based on student feedback.
  • Hold one student problem-solving circle each month and document at least one solution or follow-up action each quarter.
  • Give students an opportunity to suggest solutions to at least one site-wide concern each quarter and document the staff response.
  • Use monthly student check-ins to identify concerns about behavior, conflict, or fairness and document actions taken in response.
  • Use a student-centered problem-solving process for identified program concerns and review the results with students at least once each quarter.

Student-Led Program Improvement

Let students take a bigger role in identifying what should change, helping put a solution into action, and reflecting on whether it worked.

Possible SMART Goal

By May 2027, complete at least three student-led program improvements that strengthen voice, belonging, safety, or inclusion.

See 5 possible action ideas
  • Have students identify and help address at least one program improvement each quarter and document the recommendation and resulting change.
  • Complete at least three student-led improvement projects in which students identify a need, suggest a solution, help implement it, and reflect on the result.
  • Establish a student improvement team that meets once each month, recommends changes, and follows up on what happened.
  • Conduct a quarterly program review with students and implement at least one student-selected improvement each quarter.
  • Use a “You Said, We Did” process at least once each quarter and document the program change that resulted from student feedback.

Does your SMART goal look like one of these?

These are common patterns that sound reasonable at first but need another pass.

Too vague

Needs revision

“Improve Youth Voice and Leadership.”

Why: It does not say what will change, how progress will be measured, when it will happen, or how SSE connects.

Stronger

“By May 2027, strengthen student voice and trust through monthly feedback and four documented student-informed program improvements.”

Activities instead of a goal

Needs revision

“Hold student council meetings, interview students, meet with the principal, and create new clubs.”

Why: These are things the site might do. They belong in the Action Steps section, not as the result the site wants to achieve.

Stronger

“By May 2027, strengthen student voice and belonging through monthly feedback and four student-informed program improvements.”

Several goals bundled together

Needs revision

“Improve safety, start a student council, increase enrollment, hold parent meetings, and improve student behavior.”

Why: This is several initiatives and outcomes at once. The plan will be hard to follow and the action steps may point in different directions.

Stronger

“By May 2027, strengthen student voice and belonging through monthly student input and four documented program improvements.” Move the strategies into 3–5 action steps.

Only one standard is clear

Needs revision

“Students will have more leadership opportunities.”

Why: The YVL connection is clear, but the goal does not yet show how the work supports a safe and supportive environment.

Stronger

“By May 2027, strengthen student leadership and belonging through two supported leadership opportunities each month.”

Are your action steps specific enough?

A good action step should describe something someone can actually do and later verify.

A label is not an action

Too vague

“Student Council”

More actionable

“Establish a student advisory group by October and hold one meeting each month to gather student ideas and concerns.”

Too broad to verify

Too vague

“Improve student voice.”

More actionable

“Provide students with at least one structured opportunity each month to give feedback about activities and program climate, then review the feedback with staff.”

Responsibility and timing are unclear

Needs revision

Person Responsible: “All Staff”   |   Completion Date: “Ongoing”

Why: These can sometimes be accurate, but they make accountability difficult when a specific lead or milestone is possible.

More specific

Person Responsible: “Site Lead, with activity leaders”   |   Completion Date: “Advisory group established by October 15; meetings monthly through May.”

The step does not advance the goal

Check alignment

Goal: increase student voice and belonging. Action step: “Promote the program to increase enrollment.”

Why: Enrollment may be worthwhile, but this action does not clearly move the stated SMART goal forward.

Better test

Ask: “If we complete this action, will it clearly help us reach the SMART goal?” If the answer is no, revise or remove the step.

Quick test: Could someone ask, “Did this action happen?” and answer yes or no using evidence? If not, make the step more specific.

Try It: Is It SMART Enough?

Use this short practice to spot common problems before you enter your real Action Plan in the BTB Portal.

Before You Start

Your STAR plan should tell one clear story: two required standards, one SMART goal, and 3–5 specific action steps.

Choose only these two standards Safe & Supportive Environment (SSE) + Youth Voice & Leadership (YVL)
Write one SMART goal The goal should describe the improvement you want to achieve and connect both standards.
Add 3–5 action steps Action steps describe what people will actually do to reach the goal.
Answer portal questions literally If BTB asks what support you need from BTB, say what support you need from BTB.
Keep the timeframes straight: Your SMART goal should include a clear timeframe. The BTB Action Steps table also has a separate Completion Date field for the deadline or milestone for each individual action step.

10-second check before you move on

Your plan should read like one connected story from the goal through the action steps.

  • I selected only Safe & Supportive Environment and Youth Voice & Leadership for this STAR plan.
  • I wrote one SMART goal that connects both standards.
  • My SMART goal describes a result, not a list of activities.
  • My goal includes something I can measure or observe and a timeframe.
  • I have 3–5 specific action steps.
  • Someone could determine later whether each action step actually happened.
  • Each action step clearly helps advance my SMART goal.
  • Each step identifies who is responsible and includes a meaningful timeline.
